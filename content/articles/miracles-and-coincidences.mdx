# Miracles and Coincidences

## Defining the Extraordinary

We often speak of miracles and coincidences as if they belong to different worlds. A miracle feels sacred, while a coincidence feels ordinary. Yet sometimes, when life becomes quiet enough, the line between them begins to blur.  
A miracle is not always a sea splitting in half or the heavens opening above us. Sometimes a miracle is much smaller. It is finding the strength to get out of bed after a difficult night, or hearing the exact words you needed from a stranger who has no idea what battle you are fighting. It is arriving a few minutes late and unknowingly avoiding something that could have changed your life forever. The world rarely announces its miracles; they arrive softly, often disguised as ordinary moments.

## The Mystery of Intersection

Coincidences, on the other hand, are life's mysterious intersections. They are the unexpected meetings, the forgotten songs that suddenly play at the right moment, or the old friend who calls when you were just thinking about them. They seem random, unplanned, and meaningless to everyone except the person experiencing them. Perhaps that is why coincidences feel so special—because they whisper the question: "What if this happened for a reason?"  
No one truly knows the answer. Maybe some coincidences are simply chance, and maybe some miracles are events we do not yet understand. Or maybe life is far more interconnected than we imagine. Perhaps every person we meet, every delay we curse, and every wrong turn we take is quietly weaving itself into a larger story that only becomes visible years later.

## The Power of Hindsight

When we look back, the puzzle pieces suddenly fit. The rejection that guided us elsewhere, the goodbye that made room for a new beginning, and the unexpected encounter that changed everything—at the time, they looked like coincidences, but in hindsight, they feel like miracles. Life has a beautiful habit of revealing meaning only after we have walked through confusion. That is why faith is not always about believing in extraordinary things; sometimes faith is simply trusting that today's unanswered questions may become tomorrow's explanations.  
The heart understands this better than the mind. While the mind seeks proof, the heart seeks connection. Somewhere between proof and connection, between logic and wonder, miracles and coincidences meet.

## Everyday Wonders

Perhaps miracles are not rare at all; perhaps they happen every day. A mother waiting for her child to return home, a friend staying when everyone else leaves, or a sunrise appearing after a night that felt endless—we call them ordinary because they happen often. Yet, if they disappeared, we would call them miracles. That may be the secret: miracles are not always events that break the laws of nature, but moments that break the walls around our hearts.

## Embracing the Mystery

And coincidences? Perhaps they are gentle reminders that life is still full of mystery—a mystery that cannot always be solved, only felt. So the next time something unexpected happens, do not rush to label it. Maybe it is merely a coincidence, or maybe it is a miracle wearing ordinary clothes.
